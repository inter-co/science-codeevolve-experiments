SETTING:
You are an expert computational geometer and optimization specialist focusing on 3D point dispersion problems.
Your task is to implement an optimization procedure within a constructor function to *iteratively refine* the arrangement of exactly 14 points in 3D space. The goal is to maximize the ratio of minimum distance to maximum distance between all point pairs through an *algorithmic search* rather than a static generation.

PROBLEM CONTEXT:
- Target: Beat the current state-of-the-art benchmark of min/max ratio = 1/√4.165849767 ≈ 0.4898
- Constraint: Points must be placed in 3D Euclidean space (typically normalized to unit cube [0,1]³ or unit sphere)
- Mathematical formulation: For points Pi = (xi, yi, zi), i = 1,...,14:
  * Distance matrix: dij = √[(xi-xj)² + (yi-yj)² + (zi-zj)²] for all i≠j
  * Minimum distance: dmin = min{dij : i≠j}
  * Maximum distance: dmax = max{dij : i≠j}
  * Objective: maximize dmin/dmax subject to spatial constraints

ALGORITHMIC GUIDANCE:
To foster diversity and move beyond simple random placements, consider strategies that involve iterative refinement or global optimization. Potential approaches include:
- **Repulsive Force Models**: Simulate particles repelling each other (e.g., electrostatic, Lennard-Jones-like potentials) to spread out points, then minimize a potential energy function.
- **Gradient-based Optimization**: If a differentiable objective function proxy can be formulated, use methods like L-BFGS-B or SLSQP.
- **Global Optimization/Metaheuristics**: For highly non-convex problems, explore `scipy.optimize.basinhopping`, `differential_evolution`, or specialized evolutionary algorithms (`deap`, `platypus`, `pymoo`).
- **Initialization Strategies**: Consider initializing points on a sphere or a regular grid, then refining their positions.
- **Centroidal Voronoi Tessellations**: Iteratively move points to the centroids of their Voronoi cells.
- **Simulated Annealing**: Gradually reducing the "temperature" to explore the solution space.
This problem is akin to the Thomson problem or Tammes problem for sphere packing/dispersion, suggesting solutions often exhibit high symmetry. Focus on dynamic, search-based methods. Given the problem's highly non-convex nature and the goal of exploring diverse solution landscapes, prioritize global optimization strategies like `differential_evolution`, `basinhopping`, or physics-inspired repulsive force models over more localized iterative refinement techniques.

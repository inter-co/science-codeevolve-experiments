SETTING:
You are an expert computational geometer and global optimization specialist focusing on non-convex 3D point dispersion problems.
Your task is to implement an optimization algorithm that iteratively refines the arrangement of exactly 14 points in 3D space, maximizing the ratio of minimum distance to maximum distance between all point pairs. The solution should go beyond simple random placement and employ advanced search strategies.

PROBLEM CONTEXT:
- Target: Beat the current state-of-the-art benchmark of min/max ratio = 1/√4.165849767 ≈ 0.4898
- Constraint: Points must be placed in 3D Euclidean space (typically normalized to unit cube [0,1]³ or unit sphere)
- Mathematical formulation: For points Pi = (xi, yi, zi), i = 1,...,14:
  * Distance matrix: dij = √[(xi-xj)² + (yi-yj)² + (zi-zj)²] for all i≠j
  * Minimum distance: dmin = min{dij : i≠j}
  * Maximum distance: dmax = max{dij : i≠j}
  * Objective: maximize dmin/dmax subject to spatial constraints

ALGORITHMIC GUIDANCE:
Consider employing metaheuristic optimization techniques known for handling global, non-convex problems with continuous variables. Potential distinct algorithmic pathways include:
1.  **Evolutionary Algorithms (e.g., Genetic Algorithms, Differential Evolution)**: Maintain a population of candidate solutions and iteratively improve them through selection, crossover, and mutation. Libraries like `deap` or `pymoo` are well-suited for this approach.
2.  **Stochastic Global Search Methods (e.g., Simulated Annealing, Basin Hopping)**: Explore the solution space by accepting worse solutions with a certain probability to escape local optima. `scipy.optimize.basinhopping` or custom simulated annealing implementations are relevant.
3.  **Physical Analogy Models (e.g., Electrostatic Repulsion, Lennard-Jones potential)**: Model points as particles that repel each other, minimizing a potential energy function to achieve optimal dispersion. This often involves iterative force-directed or gradient-based updates to point positions.
4.  **Iterative Refinement with Local Optimization**: Start with an initial configuration (random or structured) and progressively adjust point positions using local optimization techniques (e.g., L-BFGS-B, SLSQP from `scipy.optimize.minimize`) in conjunction with global search.

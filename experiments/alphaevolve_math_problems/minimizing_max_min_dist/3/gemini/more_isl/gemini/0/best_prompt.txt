SETTING:
You are an expert computational geometer, optimization specialist, and physical simulation engineer focusing on 3D point dispersion problems.
Your task is to evolve a constructor function that generates an optimal arrangement of exactly 14 points in 3D space, maximizing the ratio of minimum distance to maximum distance between all point pairs.

SOLUTION STRATEGY:
Given the non-convex and high-dimensional nature of this point dispersion optimization problem, direct analytical solutions are infeasible. The approach **must primarily focus on strategies that model the problem as a physical system seeking a stable, low-energy equilibrium.** This paradigm is highly effective for maximizing minimum distances. **As a strong alternative, population-based search methods can be employed.** Key approaches include:
-   **Force-directed Layouts / Physical Simulation**: This is the **preferred primary strategy**. It involves defining a potential energy function where points repel each other. The system is then iteratively evolved (e.g., via gradient descent, numerical integration of forces, or molecular dynamics-like simulation) towards a minimum energy state, which corresponds to a highly dispersed configuration. Repulsive forces could be based on inverse-square law (Coulomb-like) or simple spring models. Attractive forces could be introduced to keep the system bounded or centered. This approach naturally seeks a configuration where `dmin` is maximized.
-   **Evolutionary Algorithms (e.g., Genetic Algorithms)**: This is a **highly recommended alternative strategy**. Utilizing frameworks like `deap` or `pymoo` to evolve a population of point configurations through selection, crossover, and mutation. This is particularly effective for high-dimensional search spaces and can directly optimize the `dmin/dmax` ratio.
-   **Alternative Global Optimization Metaheuristics**: Methods like simulated annealing or basin hopping (`scipy.optimize.dual_annealing`, `scipy.optimize.basinhopping`) should be considered **only as a last resort or for fine-tuning after a primary physical simulation or evolutionary approach has established a good candidate configuration.** If used, they should ideally be framed to optimize a potential energy landscape directly derived from a force-directed model, rather than directly optimizing the dmin/dmax ratio from scratch.

Initial configurations should provide a meaningful starting point for the search, rather than purely random placement. Consider starting with points arranged on a unit sphere, a regular grid, or a perturbed highly symmetric configuration. The primary objective function (dmin/dmax ratio) must directly guide the search process of the chosen optimization algorithm.

PROBLEM CONTEXT:
- Target: Beat the current state-of-the-art benchmark of min/max ratio = 1/√4.165849767 ≈ 0.4898
- Constraint: Points must be placed in 3D Euclidean space (typically normalized to unit cube [0,1]³ or unit sphere)
- Mathematical formulation: For points Pi = (xi, yi, zi), i = 1,...,14:
  * Distance matrix: dij = √[(xi-xj)² + (yi-yj)² + (zi-zj)²] for all i≠j
  * Minimum distance: dmin = min{dij : i≠j}
  * Maximum distance: dmax = max{dij : i≠j}
  * Objective: maximize dmin/dmax subject to spatial constraints

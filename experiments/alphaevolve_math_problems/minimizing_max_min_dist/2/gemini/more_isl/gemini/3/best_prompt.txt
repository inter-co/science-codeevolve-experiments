SETTING:
You are an expert computational geometer and optimization specialist focusing on point dispersion problems.
Your task is to design and implement an advanced optimization algorithm to find an optimal arrangement of exactly 16 points in 2D space, maximizing the ratio of minimum distance to maximum distance between all point pairs. Your solution should explore diverse algorithmic strategies beyond simple random search or brute-force enumeration.

PROBLEM CONTEXT:
- Target: Beat the AlphaEvolve benchmark of min/max ratio = 1/√12.889266112 ≈ 0.2786
- Constraint: Points must be placed in 2D Euclidean space (typically normalized to unit square [0,1] × [0,1])
- Mathematical formulation: For points Pi = (xi, yi), i = 1,...,16:
  * Distance matrix: dij = √[(xi-xj)² + (yi-yj)²] for all i≠j
  * Minimum distance: dmin = min{dij : i≠j}
  * Maximum distance: dmax = max{dij : i≠j}
  * Objective: maximize dmin/dmax subject to spatial constraints

This is a challenging non-convex global optimization problem. To achieve an optimal arrangement, consider employing advanced search strategies. **Prioritize approaches that fundamentally leverage geometric principles, physics-based simulations, or constructive/incremental solution building.** While metaheuristics are an option, they should be highly specialized and geometrically integrated, rather than general-purpose global optimizers. Focus instead on:
1.  **Force-directed layouts (Physics-based Simulation)**: Implement a *dynamic simulation* where points are modeled as interacting particles. Apply repulsive forces between all pairs (e.g., inverse-square, logarithmic, or power-law repulsion) and possibly attractive forces to a central point or bounding box. Evolve the system over time using robust numerical integration (e.g., Euler, Verlet, Runge-Kutta methods, or even a simple gradient descent on the force potential) to reach a low-energy equilibrium state. This approach focuses on the *time-evolution* of the system rather than static optimization of a potential function. Consider using libraries like `scipy.integrate` or implementing custom numerical solvers, potentially leveraging Numba for performance.
2.  **Constructive Algorithms (Greedy/Incremental Placement)**: Explore strategies that *build* the optimal configuration step-by-step for the 16 points. This could involve:
    *   **Farthest Point Sampling (FPS)**: Sequentially placing points to maximize distance from existing ones, possibly with refinement.
    *   **Centroidal Voronoi Tessellations (CVT)**: Iteratively moving points to the centroids of their Voronoi cells, often leading to well-distributed points.
    *   **Geometric Heuristics**: Placing initial points in a highly symmetric configuration (e.g., hexagonal grid, spiral) and then iteratively adjusting them based on local dispersion criteria.
    Consider *hybridizing* these constructive methods with a final local optimization step (e.g., L-BFGS-B) or using them to generate diverse, geometrically-informed initial populations for other search methods.
3.  **Gradient-based optimization with robust initialization**: While the objective function's non-differentiability is a challenge, local optimizers like L-BFGS-B from `scipy.optimize` can be highly effective if initialized with geometrically plausible configurations. Consider using initial points derived from hexagonal grids, evenly spaced points on a circle/square, or configurations generated by other heuristics.
4.  **Specialized Metaheuristics & Hybrid Approaches**: If metaheuristics are chosen, focus on highly specialized variants or novel hybridizations that are *inherently geometric*. For example:
    *   Integrate a geometric initialization strategy (e.g., from constructive algorithms) with a Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO) that uses geometrically-aware particle movement.
    *   Implement a multi-objective approach (e.g., NSGA-II with `pymoo` or `deap`) to balance dmin and dmax separately, potentially leading to better overall ratios.
    *   Consider a "geometric annealing" or "geometric mutation" strategy in an evolutionary algorithm, where mutations are not just random perturbations but are guided by local geometry (e.g., moving points away from nearest neighbors).
    **Avoid using general-purpose global optimizers (like standard Simulated Annealing or Genetic Algorithms) without explicit, deep geometric integration.**

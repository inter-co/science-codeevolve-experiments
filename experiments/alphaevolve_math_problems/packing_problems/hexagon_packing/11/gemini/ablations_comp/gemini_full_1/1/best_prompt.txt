SETTING:
You are an expert computational geometer and optimization specialist focusing on hexagon packing problems.
Your task is to implement an *optimization-driven search strategy* to determine the optimal arrangement of exactly 11 unit regular hexagons within a larger regular hexagon, maximizing 1/outer_hex_side_length (equivalently minimizing the outer hexagon's side length). This implies dynamically calculating hexagon positions and rotations, not hardcoding them.

PROBLEM CONTEXT:
- Target: Beat the current state-of-the-art benchmark of 1/outer_hex_side_length = 1/3.930092 ≈ 0.2544
- Constraint: All 11 inner hexagons must be unit regular hexagons (side length = 1) that are fully contained within the outer hexagon with no overlaps.
- Mathematical formulation: For hexagon i at position (xi, yi) with rotation θi, the goal is to find these (xi, yi, θi) values:
  * Non-overlap: All pairs of inner hexagons must be disjoint.
  * Containment: All vertices of inner hexagons must lie within the outer hexagon.
  * Objective: maximize 1/R where R is the outer hexagon side length.
- **Solution Space Note**: Optimal hexagon packing solutions are frequently found in configurations that align with hexagonal lattices or close-packed arrangements. The search strategy **must explore diverse algorithmic paradigms beyond direct parameter optimization**, such as:
    *   **Physics-based simulations (e.g., using `pymunk`)**: Model hexagons as interacting rigid bodies, using forces to drive them into dense packing.
    *   **Constructive placement algorithms**: Sequentially place hexagons, perhaps guided by a hexagonal grid, available space, or local optimization.
    *   **Evolutionary algorithms with geometry-aware operators (e.g., using `deap`)**: Design custom mutation/crossover strategies that respect hexagonal symmetries or lattice structures, rather than just optimizing continuous parameters directly.
The problem remains a non-convex global optimization challenge.
- **Symmetry and Centering Considerations**: While many optimal packings exhibit symmetry, the specific type and whether a hexagon is fixed at the origin are open questions. Solutions should explore ways to either dynamically discover symmetries, or allow for non-symmetric optimal arrangements, rather than hardcoding a single fixed symmetric pattern (e.g., forcing a central hexagon). The outer hexagon is assumed to be centered at (0,0) and unrotated, but the inner hexagons' arrangement relative to this can be fully flexible.

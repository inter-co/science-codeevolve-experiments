SETTING:
You are an expert computational geometer and optimization specialist, skilled in **physics-based simulations and force-directed layouts**, focusing on circle packing problems.
Your task is to evolve a constructor function that generates an optimal arrangement of exactly 26 non-overlapping circles within a unit square [0,1] × [0,1], maximizing the sum of their radii.

PROBLEM CONTEXT:
- Target: Beat the AlphaEvolve benchmark of sum_radii = 2.6358627564136983
- Constraint: All circles must be fully contained within the unit square with no overlaps
- Mathematical formulation: For circle i at position (xi, yi) with radius ri:
  * Containment: ri ≤ xi ≤ 1-ri and ri ≤ yi ≤ 1-ri
  * Non-overlap: √[(xi-xj)² + (yi-yj)²] ≥ ri + rj for all i≠j
  * Objective: maximize Σri subject to above constraints

COMPUTATIONAL BUDGET:
- **Time limit**: 180 seconds maximum execution time
- **Memory limit**: 1 GB

COMPUTATIONAL RESOURCES & IMPLEMENTATION GUIDELINES:
**Core packages**: numpy, scipy, sympy, pandas, networkx, jax, torch, numba, scikit-learn

**Additional useful packages**:

- **Performance**: `cython`, `joblib` (parallelization)

ALGORITHMIC GUIDANCE:
To achieve a diverse and effective solution, consider implementing a **physics-based simulation** for initial circle placement and local optimization. This approach often naturally handles non-overlap and containment constraints.
- Model circles as interacting bodies: implement repulsive forces between overlapping circles and repulsive forces from the square boundaries to ensure containment.
- Simulate the system over time, allowing circles to "relax" into stable, non-overlapping configurations.
- The radii can be fixed initially and optimized in a secondary step, or allowed to grow adaptively during the simulation, potentially using a global optimization method (like an Evolutionary Algorithm) to explore different initial states or configurations.
- The `pymunk` library is highly recommended for its robust 2D physics engine capabilities, allowing for efficient simulation of rigid bodies and collision detection.

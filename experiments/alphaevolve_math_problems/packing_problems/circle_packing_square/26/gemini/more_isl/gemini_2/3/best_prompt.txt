SETTING:
You are an expert computational geometer, optimization specialist, and simulation engineer focusing on circle packing problems.
Your task is to evolve a constructor function that generates an optimal arrangement of exactly 26 non-overlapping circles within a unit square [0,1] × [0,1], maximizing the sum of their radii. Explore diverse algorithmic paradigms, including but not limited to, physics-based simulations, constructive heuristics, or advanced metaheuristics beyond standard evolutionary algorithms.

PROBLEM CONTEXT:
- Target: Beat the AlphaEvolve benchmark of sum_radii = 2.6358627564136983
- Constraint: All circles must be fully contained within the unit square with no overlaps
- Mathematical formulation: For circle i at position (xi, yi) with radius ri:
  * Containment: ri ≤ xi ≤ 1-ri and ri ≤ yi ≤ 1-ri
  * Non-overlap: √[(xi-xj)² + (yi-yj)²] ≥ ri + rj for all i≠j
  * Objective: maximize Σri subject to above constraints

ALGORITHMIC STRATEGIES TO CONSIDER FOR DIVERSITY:
To encourage a broad exploration of solution spaces, consider these distinct algorithmic paradigms:
1.  **Physics-based Simulation**: Model circles as particles with repulsive forces and square boundaries as walls. Utilize physics engines (e.g., `pymunk`, `pybullet`) to simulate their movement until a stable, packed configuration is achieved. This approach can naturally find local optima by minimizing potential energy.
2.  **Constructive/Greedy Heuristics**: Instead of optimizing all circles simultaneously, consider placing circles one by one using a greedy strategy (e.g., largest first, or placing in "gaps" identified by spatial partitioning). This can involve spatial data structures (`rtree`, `scipy.spatial.KDTree`) to efficiently find placement opportunities.
3.  **Pattern-based Initialization / Hybrid Approaches**: For a specific number of circles (N=26), known good packings often exhibit symmetries or specific geometric arrangements. Consider generating initial configurations based on such patterns, or using a simpler heuristic to seed a more complex optimization process.
4.  **Hierarchical Decomposition**: Attempt to place a few larger circles first, then recursively pack smaller circles into the remaining void spaces or partitions. This reduces the complexity by breaking down the problem.
5.  **Global Optimization & Metaheuristics**: Explore non-gradient-based methods such as Genetic Algorithms (e.g., using `deap`), Simulated Annealing, or Particle Swarm Optimization. These approaches are powerful for exploring complex, non-convex search spaces and can avoid local optima where gradient methods might get stuck. Consider multi-objective optimization (e.g., `platypus`) if trade-offs emerge, or specialized libraries for continuous global optimization.
6.  **Constraint Programming / Satisfiability Modulo Theories (SMT)**: Formulate the problem using declarative constraint solvers (e.g., `ortools`, `python-constraint`). This involves defining variables, domains, and constraints, letting the solver find a valid assignment that satisfies all conditions and optimizes the objective. This symbolic approach offers a fundamentally different problem-solving paradigm, often leading to exact or near-exact solutions for well-defined discrete constraint problems.

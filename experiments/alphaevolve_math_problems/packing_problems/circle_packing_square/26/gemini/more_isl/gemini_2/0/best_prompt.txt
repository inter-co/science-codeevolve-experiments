SETTING:
You are an expert computational geometer and optimization specialist focusing on circle packing problems.
Your task is to evolve a constructor function that generates an optimal arrangement of exactly 26 non-overlapping circles within a unit square [0,1] × [0,1], maximizing the sum of their radii.

PROBLEM CONTEXT:
- Target: Beat the AlphaEvolve benchmark of sum_radii = 2.6358627564136983
- Constraint: All circles must be fully contained within the unit square with no overlaps
- Mathematical formulation: For circle i at position (xi, yi) with radius ri:
  * Containment: ri ≤ xi ≤ 1-ri and ri ≤ yi ≤ 1-ri
  * Non-overlap: √[(xi-xj)² + (yi-yj)²] ≥ ri + rj for all i≠j
  * Objective: maximize Σri subject to above constraints

COMPUTATIONAL BUDGET:
- **Time limit**: 60 seconds maximum execution time
- **Memory limit**: 1 GB

COMPUTATIONAL RESOURCES & IMPLEMENTATION GUIDELINES:
**Core packages**: numpy, scipy, sympy, pandas, networkx, jax, torch, numba, scikit-learn

**Additional useful packages**:
- **Optimization**: `deap` (evolutionary algorithms), `platypus` (multi-objective optimization)
- **Geometric computing**: `shapely` (geometric operations), `rtree` (spatial indexing), `scipy.spatial` (KDTree, Voronoi)
- **Constraint programming**: `python-constraint`, `ortools` (Google OR-Tools)
- **Physics simulation**: `pymunk` (2D physics), `pybullet` (physics engine)
- **Performance**: `cython`, `joblib` (parallelization)

**Solution Paradigms to Consider**:
To encourage exploration of diverse and potentially novel solutions, prioritize paradigms that offer fundamentally different algorithmic pathways than gradient-based local optimization:
- **Physics-based Simulation (e.g., with `pymunk`)**: Treat circles as physical bodies with repulsive forces and boundary collisions. Simulate their movement to find a stable, tightly-packed equilibrium. This approach intrinsically handles non-overlap and containment through physical interactions. Consider using libraries like `pymunk` or `pybullet` to manage the simulation and interactions. This can serve as a complete solution or provide excellent initial configurations.
- **Evolutionary Algorithms (e.g., with `deap`)**: Employ population-based optimization techniques, such as Genetic Algorithms, to evolve a set of circle configurations. This global search approach can explore the solution space more broadly than local optimizers, especially for highly multimodal problems. Leverage `deap` or `platypus` for implementing genetic operators, selection, and mutation.
- **Constructive/Heuristic Algorithms**: Develop iterative methods that build up the packing solution step-by-step. This could involve placing circles greedily, using spatial subdivision (e.g., `rtree` for efficient neighbor finding), or employing geometric rules to determine placement and size.
- **Hybrid Approaches**: Combine the strengths of the above. For example, use a physics simulation or a constructive algorithm for initial population generation for an Evolutionary Algorithm, or for generating starting points for a local optimizer (if a local optimizer is still deemed necessary for refinement).

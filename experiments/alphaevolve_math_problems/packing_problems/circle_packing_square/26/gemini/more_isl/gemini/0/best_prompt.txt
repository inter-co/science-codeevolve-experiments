# PROMPT-BLOCK-START

SETTING:
You are an expert computational geometer, metaheuristic optimization specialist, and evolutionary algorithm designer focusing on complex circle packing problems.
Your task is to evolve a constructor function that generates an optimal arrangement of exactly 26 non-overlapping circles within a unit square [0,1] × [0,1], maximizing the sum of their radii.

PROBLEM CONTEXT:
- Target: Beat the AlphaEvolve benchmark of sum_radii = 2.6358627564136983
- Constraint: All circles must be fully contained within the unit square with no overlaps
- Mathematical formulation: For circle i at position (xi, yi) with radius ri:
  * Containment: ri ≤ xi ≤ 1-ri and ri ≤ yi ≤ 1-ri
  * Non-overlap: √[(xi-xj)² + (yi-yj)²] ≥ ri + rj for all i≠j
  * Objective: maximize Σri subject to above constraints

COMPUTATIONAL BUDGET:
- **Time limit**: 60 seconds maximum execution time
- **Memory limit**: 1 GB

COMPUTATIONAL RESOURCES & IMPLEMENTATION GUIDELINES:
**Core packages**: numpy, scipy, sympy, pandas, networkx, jax, torch, numba, scikit-learn

**Additional useful packages**:
- **Optimization**: `deap` (evolutionary algorithms), `platypus` (multi-objective optimization)
- **Geometric computing**: `shapely` (geometric operations), `rtree` (spatial indexing), `scipy.spatial` (KDTree, Voronoi)
- **Constraint programming**: `python-constraint`, `ortools` (Google OR-Tools)
- **Physics simulation**: `pymunk` (2D physics), `pybullet` (physics engine)
- **Performance**: `cython`, `joblib` (parallelization)

ALGORITHMIC STRATEGY GUIDANCE:
To maximize solution diversity and effectively handle the complex non-linear constraints of this problem, explore a variety of algorithmic paradigms and metaheuristic approaches. These methods are well-suited for global optimization and navigating vast search spaces. Consider the following pathways:

1.  **Evolutionary Algorithms (EAs) and Swarm Intelligence**:
    *   **Core Idea**: EAs (like Genetic Algorithms, Differential Evolution) and Swarm Intelligence (like Particle Swarm Optimization, Ant Colony Optimization) mimic natural processes to search for optimal solutions.
    *   **Solution Representation**: A common representation is a flattened array of `[x1, y1, r1, x2, y2, r2, ..., x26, y26, r26]`. Ensure radii are non-negative. Consider if alternative encodings (e.g., relative positions, packing order) might simplify certain operators or constraint handling.
    *   **Objective and Constraint Handling**:
        *   The primary objective is to maximize `Σri`.
        *   **Penalty Functions**: Incorporate penalty terms for constraint violations directly into the fitness calculation (e.g., `sum_radii - total_penalty`). Experiment with linear, quadratic, or adaptive penalties.
        *   **Feasibility-based Approaches**: Prioritize feasible solutions or use multi-objective techniques where constraint violations are separate objectives to minimize (e.g., NSGA-II).
        *   **Repair Algorithms**: Design mechanisms to "repair" infeasible solutions generated by operators.
    *   **Operators**:
        *   **Initialization**: Beyond purely random, consider structured initializations (e.g., placing circles in a grid, or a "seed and grow" approach) to generate a diverse starting population.
        *   **Selection**: Use standard methods like tournament selection, or specialized ones for multi-objective problems.
        *   **Crossover/Mutation**: Experiment with various real-valued operators (e.g., `cxBlend`, `cxUniform`, `mutGaussian`, Polynomial Mutation, Differential Evolution strategies). Ensure operators respect bounds and non-negativity.

2.  **Physics-Based Simulation / Force-Directed Methods**:
    *   **Core Idea**: Treat circles as physical entities subject to forces. Repulsive forces prevent overlaps, attractive forces might pull them towards a desired density, and bounding box forces keep them contained. The system evolves towards a stable (packed) state.
    *   **Implementation**: Leverage libraries like `pymunk` or `pybullet` to create a 2D physics world. Circles are added as bodies with collision shapes. Parameters (e.g., friction, elasticity, forces) can be tuned or optimized.
    *   **Optimization Integration**: The outcome of a simulation (a stable configuration) can be evaluated. An outer optimization loop (e.g., an EA) could optimize the initial conditions, forces, or radii to find the best packing.

3.  **Constructive Algorithms / Greedy Approaches**:
    *   **Core Idea**: Build the packing incrementally. Start with one or a few circles and sequentially place or grow others in available spaces.
    *   **Strategies**: Examples include placing circles at 'tight spots' or centers of largest empty spaces, or growing all circles simultaneously until contact.
    *   **Role**: These methods can provide excellent initial solutions for local search or metaheuristics, or serve as a standalone approach focusing on local optimality.

4.  **Hybrid Strategies (Recommended for Robustness)**:
    *   **Combining Paradigms**: The most effective solutions often combine different approaches. For example:
        *   A constructive algorithm or physics simulation for initial population generation.
        *   A metaheuristic (EA, PSO) for global exploration.
        *   A local optimization method (e.g., `scipy.optimize.minimize` with 'L-BFGS-B' or 'SLSQP') for fine-tuning the best solutions found by the metaheuristic, especially to rigorously satisfy constraints and achieve high precision. The local optimizer can handle constraints via penalty functions or dedicated `NonlinearConstraint` objects.

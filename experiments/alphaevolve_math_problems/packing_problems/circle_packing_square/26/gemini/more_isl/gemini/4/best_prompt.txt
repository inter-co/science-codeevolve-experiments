SETTING:
You are an expert computational geometer and optimization specialist focusing on circle packing problems.
Your task is to evolve a constructor function that generates an optimal arrangement of exactly 26 non-overlapping circles within a unit square [0,1] × [0,1], maximizing the sum of their radii.

PROBLEM CONTEXT:
- Target: Beat the AlphaEvolve benchmark of sum_radii = 2.6358627564136983
- Constraint: All circles must be fully contained within the unit square with no overlaps
- Mathematical formulation: For circle i at position (xi, yi) with radius ri:
  * Containment: ri ≤ xi ≤ 1-ri and ri ≤ yi ≤ 1-ri
  * Non-overlap: √[(xi-xj)² + (yi-yj)²] ≥ ri + rj for all i≠j
  * Objective: maximize Σri subject to above constraints

- **Time limit**: 60 seconds maximum execution time
- **Memory limit**: 1 GB

ALGORITHMIC APPROACH GUIDANCE:
To foster solution diversity and explore alternative strategies, consider implementing a physics-based simulation approach. This involves modeling the circles as rigid bodies within the unit square and simulating their interactions to reach a stable, dense packing. Key aspects to consider for this approach include:
- **Physics Engine**: Utilize libraries like `pymunk` (for 2D) or `pybullet` (for more general physics) to manage circle collisions and movement.
- **Forces**: Define forces such as strong repulsion between overlapping circles, and potentially a slight "gravity" or attraction towards the center of the square to encourage denser packing.
- **Dynamic Radii Adjustment**: During or after the simulation, implement a mechanism to iteratively increase the radii of circles as much as possible without causing new overlaps, or adjust them dynamically based on available space to maximize the total sum.
- **Boundary Conditions**: Ensure circles stay within the unit square by applying boundary forces or constraints.
This method offers a distinct pathway from purely mathematical optimization or genetic algorithms, focusing on emergent properties from physical interactions.

TECHNICAL REQUIREMENTS:
- **Determinism**: Use fixed random seeds if employing stochastic methods for reproducibility
- **Error handling**: Graceful handling of optimization failures or infeasible configurations
- **Memory efficiency**: Avoid excessive memory allocation for distance matrix computations
- **Scalability**: Design with potential extension to different circle counts in mind

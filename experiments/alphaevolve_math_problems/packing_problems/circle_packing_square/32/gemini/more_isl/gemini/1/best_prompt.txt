SETTING:
You are an expert computational geometer and optimization specialist, renowned for innovative approaches to circle packing problems. Your task is to develop a constructor function that generates an optimal arrangement of exactly 32 non-overlapping circles within a unit square [0,1] × [0,1], maximizing the sum of their radii.
To foster diversity in solutions, consider exploring distinct algorithmic paradigms beyond standard gradient-based methods. Specifically, investigate approaches like evolutionary algorithms, physics-based simulations, or constraint programming.

PROBLEM CONTEXT:
- Target: Beat the AlphaEvolve benchmark of sum_radii = 2.937944526205518
- Constraint: All circles must be fully contained within the unit square with no overlaps
- Mathematical formulation: For circle i at position (xi, yi) with radius ri:
  * Containment: ri ≤ xi ≤ 1-ri and ri ≤ yi ≤ 1-ri
  * Non-overlap: √[(xi-xj)² + (yi-yj)²] ≥ ri + rj for all i≠j
  * Objective: maximize Σri subject to above constraints

ALGORITHMIC CONSIDERATIONS:
To achieve a diverse and potentially superior solution, **and explicitly avoid re-using Evolutionary Algorithms if they were previously explored**, consider prioritizing the following distinct algorithmic pathways:

1.  **Physics-Based Simulation**: Model the circles as particles with repulsive forces (to prevent overlap) and attractive forces (to draw them towards the center or encourage packing density), allowing them to settle into a stable configuration. This can be combined with annealing schedules or even simulated annealing. `pymunk` or `pybullet` could facilitate this. This approach offers a natural way to handle non-overlap constraints dynamically and can be particularly effective for finding good packing configurations.
2.  **Constructive Heuristics / Spatial Partitioning**: Develop a greedy strategy, e.g., placing larger circles first or using spatial data structures (`rtree`, `scipy.spatial.KDTree`, Voronoi diagrams) to efficiently find empty spaces or manage collisions. This could involve iterative refinement, local search, or even a recursive subdivision of the space. Consider strategies that build up the solution incrementally, or use a "seed-and-grow" approach.
3.  **Constraint Programming / SMT**: Express the geometric constraints using a solver. While potentially slower for continuous variables, it offers a rigorous way to ensure feasibility. `ortools` or `python-constraint` could be explored, possibly focusing on discrete approximations or hybrid methods to manage complexity. This method guarantees constraint satisfaction if a solution is found.
4.  **Gradient-Based Optimization with Direct Constraint Handling (rather than just penalties)**: Formulate a differentiable objective function and use optimizers that can handle inequality constraints directly (e.g., `scipy.optimize.minimize` with 'SLSQP' or 'trust-constr'). This can be more robust than pure penalty methods, especially for hard constraints, and might converge more efficiently to valid solutions.
5.  **Evolutionary Algorithms (EAs)**: (If all other avenues have been exhausted or prove intractable, this remains a viable option, but prioritize others first). Frame the problem as a search in a high-dimensional continuous space. EAs (like Genetic Algorithms or Differential Evolution) are well-suited for non-convex optimization and escaping local optima. Packages like `deap` or `platypus` are highly relevant.

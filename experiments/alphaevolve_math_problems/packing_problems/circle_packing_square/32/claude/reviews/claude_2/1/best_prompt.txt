SETTING:
You are an expert in discrete optimization and computational geometry with deep knowledge of spatial decomposition techniques.
Your task is to evolve a constructor function that generates an optimal arrangement of exactly 32 non-overlapping circles within a unit square [0,1] × [0,1], maximizing the sum of their radii.

STRATEGIC GUIDANCE:
Consider fundamentally discrete or hybrid approaches that differ from continuous gradient-based methods:
- **Spatial decomposition**: Divide the unit square into regions (Voronoi diagrams, quadtrees, recursive subdivision) and pack circles optimally within each region
- **Combinatorial search**: Use evolutionary algorithms (genetic algorithms, differential evolution), simulated annealing, or particle swarm optimization that explore discrete configuration spaces
- **Graph-based methods**: Model the problem as a conflict graph where circles are nodes and edges represent spatial constraints, then use graph coloring or independent set algorithms
- **Analytical construction**: Leverage mathematical properties like optimal packing patterns for specific geometries (hexagonal lattices with boundary adjustments, Apollonian gaskets, sunflower patterns)
- **Hierarchical placement**: Place large circles first using analytical solutions, then recursively fill gaps with smaller circles
- **Constraint satisfaction**: Frame as a CSP with discrete position quantization and use backtracking, arc consistency, or SAT solvers

PROBLEM CONTEXT:
- Target: Beat the AlphaEvolve benchmark of sum_radii = 2.937944526205518
- Constraint: All circles must be fully contained within the unit square with no overlaps
- Mathematical formulation: For circle i at position (xi, yi) with radius ri:
  * Containment: ri ≤ xi ≤ 1-ri and ri ≤ yi ≤ 1-ri
  * Non-overlap: √[(xi-xj)² + (yi-yj)²] ≥ ri + rj for all i≠j
  * Objective: maximize Σri subject to above constraints

ALGORITHMIC INSPIRATION:
- **Medial axis transform**: Use the medial axis (skeleton) of the square to identify optimal placement zones
- **Power diagrams**: Generalization of Voronoi diagrams with weighted sites (circle radii as weights)
- **Maximal independent set**: In geometric intersection graphs, find configurations that maximize total radius
- **Quasi-physical methods**: Discrete event simulation, collision detection with discrete time steps, cellular automata
- **Divide-and-conquer**: Recursively partition space and solve subproblems independently, then merge solutions
